What a sequence is, and which settings to use

A sequence is the timeline for one piece of work. It holds your clips, audio and effects, and it sets the frame size and frame rate of the whole edit. Match it to where the video is going from the start, and nothing stretches, stutters or fights you at export.

  • Premiere 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

Not sure what to choose? Drag your main clip onto the New Item icon at the bottom of the Project panel. Premiere creates a sequence whose frame size and frame rate match that clip.

What a sequence is

  • One project can hold many sequences, for example a full landscape cut for YouTube and a short vertical cut for Reels, both using the same footage.
  • A sequence stores two things: the edit (clips on video and audio tracks, effects, graphics) and its settings (frame size, frame rate, audio format).
  • The Editing Mode setting does not decide your final file. You choose the output format when you export.

Three ways to create a sequence

  1. From a clip (easiest)

    Drag a clip from the Project panel onto the New Item icon, or right-click the clip and choose New Sequence From Clip.

    Best when the destination matches how the camera recorded, for example 1920×1080 at 25p going to landscape YouTube.

  2. From the menu, with your own settings

    Choose File › New › Sequence (Ctrl+N / Cmd+N). Pick the closest preset, or open the Settings tab, set Editing Mode to Custom and enter the values yourself.

    Click Save Preset and give it a clear name such as "Reels 1080x1920 25p" so you can pick it next time.

  3. Drop the first clip into an empty sequence

    If the clip doesn't match the sequence, Premiere asks whether to change the sequence to match the clip or keep the existing settings. If you already set the sequence for its destination (vertical, say), keep the existing settings.

The settings that matter

SettingWhat it isWhat a beginner should pick
TimebaseThe sequence's unit of time, in other words its frame rateMatch your camera. Typically 24 for a film look, 25 for PAL regions and 29.97 for NTSC regions.
Frame SizeImage size in pixelsSet it for the destination, such as 1920×1080 or 1080×1920. The maximum is 10,240×8,192.
Pixel Aspect RatioThe shape of each pixelSquare Pixels (1.0). A mismatch stretches or squashes the picture.

Working color space / Auto Tone Map MediaColour management for the sequenceLeave the defaults. Auto Tone Map Media makes it easier to mix Log, iPhone HLG and other HDR footage in one sequence.
Audio Sample RateAudio resolution48000 Hz, the video standard.
Video PreviewsThe format used for preview renders inside PremiereLeave the default (many presets use Apple ProRes 422 LT).
Maximum Bit Depth / Maximum Render QualityHighest processing qualityTurn on only when needed; they cost RAM and render time.

Changing settings later

Choose Sequence › Sequence Settings, or right-click the sequence in the Project panel and choose Sequence Settings. When you change the frame size, tick Scale motion effects proportionally when changing frame size so clip positions and sizes follow.

Don't shrink the frame size to fix choppy playback. Lower the playback resolution in the Program Monitor instead.

Common beginner mistakes

  • 4K clips look zoomed in on a 1080 sequenceRight-click the clip in the timeline and choose Set to Frame Size. It fits the frame and you can still punch in sharply, because the source is bigger.
  • Sequence frame rate doesn't match the cameraYou get stutter or repeated frames. Creating the sequence from your main clip avoids this.
  • Editing vertical clips in a landscape sequenceYou get black bars. Make a separate vertical sequence.
  • Wrong Fields or Pixel Aspect RatioCombing lines or a stretched picture. Use No Fields and Square Pixels for ordinary footage.

Using a version older than 27.0

Premiere 25.x–26.x
  • Almost everything here applies; the main menus haven't changed.
  • Colour management options such as Auto Tone Map Media may be missing or in a different place in older versions.
  • Intel-based Macs stop at 26.x, because Premiere 27.0 dropped Intel Mac support.
