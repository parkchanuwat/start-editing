Compositions and layers

In After Effects a piece of work is a composition, with its own frame size, frame rate and duration. Inside it are layers stacked like sheets of acetate: higher layers cover what's below.

  • After Effects 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

The big difference from Premiere: a Premiere track holds many clips in a row, but an After Effects layer has only one source. That's why a single piece often has dozens of layers.

Creating a composition

  1. From footage (easiest)

    Drag a video from the Project panel onto the New Composition icon at the bottom of the panel. The comp takes that file's size and frame rate.

  2. Or set it up yourself

    Choose Composition › New Composition (Ctrl+N / Cmd+N), pick a Preset or type Width, Height, Frame Rate and Duration.

  3. Change it later

    Composition › Composition Settings (Ctrl+K / Cmd+K) lets you change duration or size at any time.

Transform: five properties every layer has

Press a letter to reveal that property on the selected layer: A Anchor Point · P Position · S Scale · R Rotation · T Opacity. Hold Shift and press another to show several at once.

Previewing

  • Press Spacebar to preview. The green bar on the timeline shows what's cached and will play smoothly.
  • Lower the Composition panel's Resolution. Half or Quarter makes previews faster while you work. It doesn't change the comp's frame size.
  • Set a work area. Press B and N to set the start and end of the range you preview.

Common beginner mistakes

  • You added something but can't see itIt's probably under an opaque layer. Drag it higher in the Timeline.
  • Mixing up Resolution and frame sizeResolution in the Composition panel is preview quality; the real size is in Composition Settings.
  • The comp is shorter than you needChange Duration in Composition Settings rather than dragging the end of a layer.
  • Editing the wrong compCheck the tab name above the Timeline before you change anything, especially with nested precomps.
