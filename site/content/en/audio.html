Audio and music

Viewers forgive rough pictures far more easily than sound they can't follow. The basics: dialogue clear, music underneath, and nothing over 0 dB. You can do most of it from the Essential Sound panel.

  • Premiere 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

Tell Premiere what each clip is first. Select clips and click Dialogue, Music, SFX or Ambience in the Essential Sound panel. The rest of the panel adapts to the type you choose.

Audio types in Essential Sound

TypeUse forCommon options
DialogueSpeech, interviews, voice-overLoudness Auto-Match, Enhance Speech, Repair, Clarity
MusicBackground musicLoudness Auto-Match, Ducking
SFXSound effects such as doors or whooshesLoudness, left/right position
AmbienceRoom tone, crowds, windLoudness, Reverb

A basic mix, step by step

  1. Keep tracks tidy

    Dialogue on A1–A2, music on A3, effects on A4, so you can select each type at once. For a layout suited to sound, switch to the Audio workspace.

  2. Set the type and Auto-Match

    Select all dialogue clips, click Dialogue, then Auto-Match under Loudness so every clip sits at a similar level. Do the same for music with Music.

  3. Clean up speech with Enhance Speech

    On the Dialogue tab, click Enhance under Enhance Speech. When processing finishes, adjust Mix Amount; if the voice sounds unnatural, lower it to blend some of the original back in.

    Enhance Speech is for speech only, not music or sound effects.

  4. Duck the music under dialogue

    Select the music, turn on Ducking, choose which audio types should trigger it (for example Dialogue), set Sensitivity, Reduce By and Fades, then click Generate Keyframes. Premiere adds keyframes that lower the music where people speak.

    You can click Generate Keyframes again any time; the old keyframes are replaced with a new set based on the current settings.

  5. Watch the Audio Meters through the whole piece

    Play from start to finish and watch the meters beside the timeline. Never touch the red at the top (0 dB). If you do, lower that clip with Audio Gain (G).

More tips

  • Check on headphones and speakers. Headphones catch noise; speakers show whether the music is too loud.
  • Change volume over time with keyframes. Ctrl-click (Windows) or Cmd-click (Mac) the volume line on a clip in the timeline to add a keyframe.
  • Generate music or sound effects in 27.0. Premiere 27.0 can generate music, sound effects and soundscapes with AI. Check the usage terms and the credits it uses first.
  • Use music you have rights to. Songs from music apps or YouTube can trigger copyright claims. Use a licensed library such as Adobe Stock.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Music drowns out the dialogueTurn on Ducking or bring the music down further; under speech it should be clearly quieter than the voice.
  • Distorted soundThe mix goes over 0 dB. Lower the Audio Gain of the loudest clip, not your computer's volume.
  • Adjusting every clip by handAuto-Match on all clips of one type at once is faster and more consistent.
  • Too much Enhance SpeechVoices can sound robotic or words can change. Lower Mix Amount and listen again.
