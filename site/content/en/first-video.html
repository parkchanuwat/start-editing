Your first video, start to finish

This page walks you through one short video in Premiere, from preparing files to a finished MP4. Follow the steps in order; each links to a page with the details. One full pass gives you a feel for the whole app.

  • Premiere 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

Running Premiere in Korean or Simplified Chinese? This site uses the English menu names. To change the app's language, open the Creative Cloud desktop app › Preferences and set Default install language (under Apps or Language, depending on the version). Then uninstall and reinstall Premiere for the change to apply.

Before you start

  • 3–10 clips from a phone or camera, and one song you have the rights to use
  • Enough free disk space; Premiere creates cache and preview files as you work
  • Decide where it is going first: landscape for YouTube or vertical for Shorts / Reels, because that sets the sequence size

8 steps

  1. Set up folders and import

    Make a folder such as MyVlog with Footage and Music inside it. Create the new project in that folder, then drag your files into the Project panel. Don't move or rename files after importing.

    Managing files and projects →

  2. Create a sequence

    Drag your main clip onto the New Item icon. For a vertical video, create a 1080×1920 sequence yourself.

    Sequences and Sequence Settings →

  3. Pick your takes

    Double-click a clip to open it in the Source Monitor, press I and O around the best part, then press , to Insert it into the timeline. Work through the clips in story order.

    Source Monitor vs Program Monitor →

  4. Edit in the timeline

    Put the music on an audio track, mark the beats with M, cut clips to the beat with the Razor, and close gaps with Ripple Delete. Play the whole thing often.

    Editing in the Timeline → · Markers →

  5. Add titles and graphics

    Open Window › Graphics Templates, drag a title template onto track V2, and edit the text in the Properties panel.

    MOGRT → · Effects and keyframes →

  6. Grade the colour

    Click the Color tab, press Auto Color clip by clip, then adjust Contrast, Exposure and Temperature so every shot matches.

    Color grading with Color Mode →

  7. Add captions

    If there is speech, transcribe it with Speech to Text, create captions, then check spelling and line length.

    Korean and Simplified Chinese captions →

  8. Export

    Click the Export tab, choose Format H.264 and Preset Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate, check frame size and frame rate, then click Export.

    Common presets and resolutions →

Habits that keep projects safe

  • Press Ctrl+S / Cmd+S often and keep Auto Save on.
  • Lock the edit before polishing. Grade, add effects and caption after the clip order settles, so you don't redo work.
  • Watch it where it will be seen. Send a vertical video to your phone before posting it.
  • Panels in a mess? Choose Window › Workspaces › Reset to Saved Layout to put them back.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Importing from Downloads or the desktop, then deleting the filesThe clips go Media Offline. Move files into the project folder before importing.
  • The sequence is the wrong orientationYou find out at export that you needed vertical. Get it right at step 2.
  • Grading or captioning before the edit is doneEvery later cut means redoing that work.
  • Using music without the rightsThe video may be muted or claimed on the platform. Choose music you can use from the start.

Using a version older than 27.0

Premiere 25.x–26.x
  • Every step is the same except grading, where older versions use the Lumetri Color panel instead of Color Mode.
