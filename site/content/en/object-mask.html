Object Mask: select a person or object in one click

Object Mask uses AI to find people and objects in your shot. One click gives you a mask, tracking keeps it on the subject through the shot, and then you apply effects inside or outside it. No rotoscoping in After Effects.

  • Premiere 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

The first use takes a moment. Premiere downloads its AI models first and Object Masking is unavailable until they finish. Watch progress in the Progress panel.

What it is good for

  • Blurring faces, licence plates or logos you don't have permission to show, which protects people's privacy.
  • Blurring the background or making it black and white so the subject stands out, using Inverted.
  • Fixing light or colour on the subject only, such as a backlit face, without touching the background.
  • Putting text behind a person. Duplicate the clip onto a higher track, change its mask to an Opacity Mask, and place the text between the two tracks.

Steps: blur the background behind a person

  1. Choose the Object Mask Tool

    In the toolbar, click and hold the mask tools group. Object Mask sits with the Ellipse, Rectangle and Pen tools.

  2. Hover, then click the subject

    Select the clip in the timeline and move the pointer over the Program Monitor. Detected objects highlight; click to select one.

    Start from the frame where the subject is most clearly visible for the best mask.

  3. If nothing highlights, draw roughly

    Switch to Rectangle or Lasso from the menu at the bottom of the Program Monitor and draw around the subject. Use the + and − buttons to add or subtract parts; hold Alt / Option to switch temporarily.

  4. Track the mask

    The new mask appears under Unassigned Masks in Effect Controls. Use the forward and backward track buttons so it follows the subject through the clip. Tracking quality can be Faster or Better.

  5. Add the effect

    Open the Effects panel and drag Gaussian Blur onto the clip. The mask attaches to that effect, so the blur applies only inside the mask.

  6. Invert and soften

    Tick Inverted to blur everything outside the mask, the background. Then adjust Feather for softer edges and Expansion to grow or shrink the mask.

  7. Deselect the mask before reviewing

    While the mask is selected you see a red overlay. Click elsewhere in Effect Controls before playing back.

Settings worth knowing

SettingWhat it doesUse it when
Sharp / SmoothSharp gives crisp edges; Smooth gives soft edgesUse Smooth for hair, fur, thin fabric or semi-transparent things
SharpnessHow much of Smooth mode's soft fringe is kept, adjusted with S-Curve or LevelsRecovering flyaway hair, or trimming background that crept in
FeatherSoftens the mask edgeBlending the effect into the shot
ExpansionNegative values shrink the mask; positive values grow itThe mask is slightly too small or too big
OpacityHow strong the mask isToning the effect down
InvertedApplies the effect outside the mask insteadBlurring or grading the background

Where mask files live

Mask data is saved in a folder named <Project Name> Masks next to the project file. Move that folder whenever you move the project. If Premiere can't find it, Object Masks show as offline in Effect Controls; click Locate and point to the folder. Other kinds of masks are not affected.

Premiere removes unused Object Mask files when you close the project, which saves disk space.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Masks disappear when someone else opens the projectThe Masks folder wasn't sent with it.
  • The picture turns red, so the effect seems wrongThat is the overlay shown while the mask is selected. Deselect it first.
  • The mask doesn't follow the subjectIt hasn't been tracked, or tracking started on a frame where the subject is blocked. Start from a clear frame and track in both directions.
  • The mask is stuck under Unassigned MasksIt isn't attached to an effect yet. Drag an effect onto the clip, or apply the effect first and then create the mask.
  • Hair edges look blockySwitch to Smooth mode and adjust Sharpness.

Using a version older than 27.0

Object Mask version history
  • Object Mask first shipped in Premiere 26.0 (January 2026). 25.x doesn't have it; you draw masks by hand and track them.
  • Smooth mode arrived later in early 2026, and 26.5 detects more objects with more accurate edges.
  • Intel Macs and Windows PCs with older AMD graphics drivers fall back to the previous model, and Premiere 27.0 no longer supports Intel Macs.
