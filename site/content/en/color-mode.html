Color grading with Color Mode

Fix clips that look too dark, flat or tinted, using Premiere's new grading workspace: open Color Mode, pick a clip, then adjust one value at a time in order.

  • Premiere 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

Following a tutorial that uses the Lumetri Color panel? Premiere 27.0 retires that panel. The Lumetri Color effect is still there and older projects graded with Lumetri open as before, but for new work use Color Mode as shown here.

Before you start

  • Use Premiere 27.0 or later. Check under About Premiere (Help menu on Windows, Premiere menu on Mac).
  • Finish the edit first, so you don't grade clips that get cut later.
  • Set the sequence's Color Management before grading. The default is Direct Rec. 709 (SDR), which suits ordinary social and YouTube videos. Choose Rec. 2100 HLG or PQ only for HDR work. Changing it after grading changes how every clip looks.

Steps

  1. Open Color Mode

    In Edit mode, open the sequence in the Timeline, then click the Color tab in the header bar at the top of the window. Premiere opens the grading workspace.

    The Project Browser is still available in Color Mode, so you can switch sequences without going back to Edit mode.

  2. Pick a clip in the Clip Grid

    The Clip Grid shows every clip in the sequence as a thumbnail, in timeline order. Click the clip you want to fix. The one outlined in white is the Current Clip, and every adjustment goes to it.

  3. Make sure you are changing one clip

    At the top of the Color Controls panel, choose an operation:

    • Clip Operation changes only the selected clip. It is chosen for you whenever you pick a new clip.
    • Sequence Style applies a look to the whole sequence. Beginners should leave it until the individual clips are done.

  4. Try Auto Color first

    The Auto Color button sits at the bottom left of the Adjust controls and sets Exposure, Temperature and Tint for you. To fix just one thing, right-click or click and hold it, then choose Auto Exposure or Auto White Balance.

    Auto Color works from the frame under the playhead, so park the playhead on a frame that represents the clip.

  5. Adjust by clicking and dragging

    Most controls change two values at once: drag up or down for the main value, left or right for the second one.

    1. Click once inside a control to latch it. A HUD appears with a graph for that control.
    2. Drag, or use the arrow keys for fine steps.
    3. Click again, or press Enter / Esc, to accept and unlatch.

    You can also press, drag and release without latching. Work through the order Adobe recommends.

  6. Compare, then move to the next clip

    Select several clips in the Clip Grid and turn on Comparison View in the Color Monitor for a split screen. Press ↓ for the next clip or ↑ for the previous one, until every clip that needs work is done.

The basic Adjust controls

Start with the Color & Contrast group, which works on the whole image. These five controls fix most beginner problems.

ControlMain value (drag up/down)Second value (drag left/right)Fixes
ContrastDifference between the brightest and darkest pixelsMoves the contrast pivot pointFlat images with no depth
ExposureOverall brightnessBlack: crush or lift the shadowsToo dark, too bright, washed out
TemperatureWarmer or coolerTint: removes a green or magenta castOrange, blue or green casts
BalanceRemixes the red, green and blue channels to push colour in any directionUse instead of Temperature when the cast is not orange or blue
SaturationRaises or lowers the intensity of all colours at onceDull or overly strong colour

The order Adobe suggests for beginners

  1. Contrast and Exposure, adjusting Black as needed
  2. Temperature or Balance, choose one
  3. Global Saturation
  4. Zones, if some tonal range still needs work. Shadows and Highlights are there by default.
  5. Saturation/Hue/Lum Shift for one specific colour, such as the sky
  6. Texture and Sharpness
  7. Style, for a stylised look

Useful shortcuts

Common beginner mistakes

  • Can't find the Lumetri Color panelIt is retired in 27.0. Use Color Mode, or for a quick fix without leaving Edit mode, use the Color section of the Properties panel.
  • Changing Color Management after gradingEvery clip shifts. Set it correctly before you start.
  • Fixing one clip changes them allYou are in Sequence Style. Check the operation name at the top of Color Controls before dragging.
  • Stacking the Lumetri Color effect on top of Color ModeAdobe recommends Color Mode alone for new projects: mixing them limits your options, and Lumetri can clip out-of-range values that can't be recovered later.
  • Pushing Zones or Hue Shift too farThe image starts to look fake. Use them sparingly and watch the HUD graph for clipping at the top or bottom.

Using a version older than 27.0

Premiere 26.x and the 25.6.x LTS line
  • These versions still have the Lumetri Color panel and no released Color Mode, so the steps on this page don't apply. See "Grading with Lumetri Color" instead.
  • Color Mode was a public beta in the Premiere (beta) app from April 2026. Its screens may differ from 27.0.
  • After updating to 27.0, projects graded with Lumetri still open, and their grades stay editable in Effect Controls.
