Keyframes in After Effects

A keyframe says "at this time, this property has this value", and After Effects fills in everything between. What makes motion look professional is the timing between keyframes, which you control with Easy Ease and the Graph Editor.

  • After Effects 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

Select keyframes and press F9. Motion then eases out and slows down before it stops, instead of shooting off and halting dead. Compare the two in the demo.

Your first keyframes

  1. Reveal the property

    Select a layer and press P for Position (or S Scale, R Rotation, T Opacity).

  2. Click the stopwatch

    Move the playhead to the start and click the stopwatch next to the property name. That's your first keyframe.

    Shortcut to add a Position keyframe: Alt+Shift+P (Windows) or Option+P (Mac).

  3. Move in time and change the value

    Move the playhead forward, then drag the layer in the Composition panel or type a new value. After Effects adds the second keyframe for you.

  4. Add Easy Ease

    Drag-select both keyframes and press F9. The keyframe icons change from diamonds to hourglass shapes.

  5. Refine in the Graph Editor

    Press Shift+F3 to open the Graph Editor. Drag a keyframe's handle further out to increase its influence, making the ease stronger.

Value graph vs speed graph

GraphShowsUse it when
Value GraphThe property's actual value over time (X red, Y green)You want to see where a value goes, or build an overshoot
Speed GraphHow fast the value changesYou want to shape speed, such as a hard start

In the Composition panel, the motion path shows one dot per frame. Dots far apart mean fast; dots close together mean slow.

Interpolation types

TypeResult
LinearConstant speed with abrupt changes at keyframes; looks stiff
Bezier / Easy EaseSmooth acceleration and deceleration; Easy Ease uses 33.33% influence by default
Auto BezierThe default for motion paths, giving smooth curves
HoldKeeps the value until the next keyframe, then jumps; good for snappy changes

Change it by right-clicking a keyframe › Keyframe Interpolation.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Changing a value changes the whole layerThe stopwatch isn't on, so there are no keyframes. Turn it on first, then change values.
  • Motion too fast or too slowDrag keyframes further apart to slow down, or closer together to speed up.
  • Easy Ease feels sluggishLower the influence in the Graph Editor, or use Ease Out on the first keyframe and Ease In on the last only.
  • Dragging handles in the Graph Editor does nothing usefulCheck whether you're in the Speed Graph or Value Graph; handles behave differently in each.

Using a version older than 27.0

After Effects 25.x–26.x
  • Keyframes, Easy Ease and the Graph Editor work the same in every version.
  • The Effect Controls panel was redesigned in 26.5, so it may look different in earlier versions.
