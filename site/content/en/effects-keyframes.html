Effects and keyframes

An effect changes how a clip looks, such as a blur, a distortion or a colour shift. A keyframe says when a value should change. Together they make things move: a title sliding in, or a shot slowly coming into focus.

  • Premiere 27.0
  • Updated 27 Nov 2026
  • Beginner
  • Based on Adobe Help Center

New to try: Premiere now includes more than 90 modern effects, transitions and animations at no extra cost. All of them are GPU-accelerated, so they play back in real time without rendering, and a Surprise Me button generates a new variation each time you click it.

Three panels to know

Every clip already has the built-in Motion (position, scale, rotation), Opacity and Time Remapping effects. You don't need to find them in the Effects panel.

Adding an effect

  1. Find the effect

    Open the Effects panel and type its name, such as Gaussian Blur.

  2. Apply it

    Drag it onto a clip in the timeline, or select the clip first and double-click the effect.

  3. Adjust it

    Select the clip and change values in Effect Controls. Click the fx icon next to the effect name to toggle it and compare before and after.

  4. Reuse it

    Right-click the effect name in Effect Controls and choose Save Preset, or copy all effects to another clip with Paste Attributes (Ctrl+Alt+V / Cmd+Opt+V).

Creating keyframes

You need at least two keyframes for a change: the first holds the starting value, the second the end value. Premiere fills in everything between them.

  1. Select the clip and open Effect Controls

    Expand the effect you want to animate, for example Motion › Position.

  2. Park the playhead at the start and click the stopwatch

    Click the Toggle Animation stopwatch next to the property. That creates the first keyframe at the playhead.

  3. Move the playhead and change the value

    Move to the end point and type a new value; the second keyframe is created for you. You can also click Add/Remove Keyframe.

  4. See and edit keyframes in the timeline

    Click the wrench icon in the timeline and choose Show Video Keyframes, then pick the property from the effect menu next to the clip name.

Interpolation: the speed between keyframes

Right-click a keyframe in Effect Controls or the timeline and choose Temporal Interpolation. Compare the types in the demo above.

TypeWhat happensUse it for
LinearChanges at a constant speed (the default)Graphics that should move evenly
Ease In / Ease OutSlows down into a keyframe / speeds up out of itNatural-looking motion; the one beginners use most
BezierDrag handles to shape the speed curve yourselfPrecise control of timing
Auto Bezier / Continuous BezierA smooth curve through the keyframe, automatic / with adjustable handlesSeveral keyframes in a row
HoldKeeps the value, then jumps at the next keyframeStepped or sudden changes

Ease In and Ease Out are Bezier curves, so the menu shows Bezier after you pick them. You didn't do anything wrong.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Clicking the stopwatch again deleted every keyframeTurning Toggle Animation off removes all keyframes for that property. Use Add/Remove Keyframe to add more.
  • Changing a value didn't make a new keyframeThe playhead was still on the old keyframe, so you edited it. Move the playhead first.
  • Mixing up Effects and Effect ControlsEffects is where you choose; Effect Controls is where you adjust. They are different panels.
  • Several heavy effects make playback stutterLower the Program Monitor's Resolution or render that section. The new GPU-accelerated effects play back more smoothly.
  • Trimming a clip broke the animationKeyframes live on the clip; trimming away part of the animation changes what is left. Animate after the edit is locked.

Using a version older than 27.0

Premiere 25.x–26.x
  • Keyframes and interpolation work the same.
  • The modern effects and transitions arrived during 26.x, and some replace older ones (Gradient replaces Ramp, for example, and a new Channel Blur replaces the legacy one). If you can't find one, check your version.
